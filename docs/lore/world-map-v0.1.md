# Gnomeworks World Map, v0.1

Master map planning document.  We will build this file one section at a time.  Only the geographic outline is included in this first version.

**Source of truth:** Nathan's hand-drawn coastline and his statement that the main island is country-sized, approximately comparable to the UK and hundreds of miles across.  [Map Geography Audit v0.1](map-geography-audit-v0.1.md) catalogs named terrain and placement clues; [Location Gazetteer v0.5](location-gazetteer-v0.5.md) supplies proposed lore and location descriptions.  Geography invented in the gazetteer remains provisional and must fit the approved coastline, never the reverse.

## 1. Geographic Outline

### 1.1. The map at country scale

The world map covers one **large main island**, several **substantial detached islands**, small groups of **coastal islets**, and the intervening seas.  The main island is hundreds of miles across; towns separated by only a finger's width on the drawing may be days apart.  Its inland areas have room for mountain ranges, rivers with long courses, forests, farms, marshes, wildlands, and settlements still unnamed.

A master map should depict the principal physical systems and regional centers.  A small shrine, single shop, roadside attraction, or cave entrance can wait for a closer regional map.  No fixed mileage, scale bar, area, coordinates, latitude, or compass rose has been approved.

**Important:** Directions here refer to the upright *page* as "top," "bottom," "left," and "right."  The page has not yet been assigned true north.

### 1.4. The first watershed skeleton

**Mountains first, rivers second.**  The main island is large enough for multiple independent drainage basins.  Mountain ridges should divide where rain falls, rivers should descend into lowlands, and major mouths should match the coastline.

1. **Western candidate:** River's Belt flows through Gnohio and eventually reaches Port Gnaru on the sea.  The Springwater and Backwater Cog waterways are related in the draft, but their exact junctions and flow directions have not been drawn.
2. **Eastern/southern candidate:** A warm Steam Stream flows from higher land toward Slanic.  We have not decided whether it feeds the main southern delta, a lake, or a separate coastal outlet.
3. **Northern candidate:** North Ham'n'Gnome mentions a northern river estuary.  It must **not automatically be joined** to River's Belt; two river systems may be more plausible.
4. **Wetland sources:** Fairy Fen, the Bog of Eternal Regret, Pixie Puddles and the Swamp of Low Visits may be parts of an extensive southern basin, separate drainage pockets, or both.
5. **Outer islands:** Each substantial island needs its own drainage, freshwater supply and coasts.  They are not miniature copies of the mainland.

The interior looping contour is especially important.  If it is confirmed as a lake, inland sea, tidal inlet or island-filled water basin, it may reshape the entire hydrologic plan.  **Its interpretation remains pending.**

### 1.5. Climate and geological coherence

A UK-scale island can carry varied climates, but we should not place snowfields, tropical coasts, temperate farms, bogs and extensive deserts in arbitrary neighboring squares.

- **Elevation** can produce snowy ridges and sheltered valleys.
- **Prevailing winds and a mountain rain shadow** could explain wet woodlands on one side and dry country on another.  The wind direction has not been selected.
- **Ocean currents and exposure** might account for fog, a warmer protected bay and contrasting offshore climates.
- **Wetlands** depend on low-lying terrain and water balance; a southern swamp should have a reason to remain wet.
- **Volcanoes** can produce ash plains, obsidian and local geothermal effects.  They do not automatically cause all the warm streams or unusual crystal sites.
- **Fantasy effects** are allowed where intentional, with a story consequence.  They should be chosen rather than invoked merely to fix an inconvenient map.

The master sketch currently has no elevation marks, rivers, trees, dunes or compass.  All such marks will be new, reviewable additions.

### 1.6. Region names and political geography

The names available for later assignment are **Gnomadica**, **Gnomerica**, **Gnohio**, **Gnafrica**, **Gnufoundland**, **Slaanilok**, **Slanic**, and **Gno Dao Cradle**.

The present gazetteer loosely associates those names with central industry, western river country, southwestern drylands, a northern cold island, an eastern archipelago, southern wetlands and eastern highlands.  Those assignments are **hypotheses**, not confirmed regions or borders.

In particular, do not label islands A–E as Gnufoundland or Slaanilok, and do not force Gnafrica onto the southwestern main-island peninsula, before reviewing the coastline and intended distribution of landmasses.  A named forest or river can cross regional boundaries; regions should follow plausible topography and settlement history rather than make the land fit an existing political outline.
